# Commercialization without closing the source

## Recommended model

### Community software
- source remains open;
- official personal-use builds remain free;
- core security features are not paywalled;
- community can audit and improve the product.

### Revenue layers
1. managed organization console;
2. enterprise policy distribution;
3. priority threat/rule feed and managed response;
4. SSO/SCIM/SIEM deployment services;
5. support/SLA;
6. SiteWarden security assessments and rollout projects;
7. commercial embedding license for vendors that do not want AGPL obligations;
8. sponsorships/grants.

## Dual licensing warning
If the project uses AGPL plus a separate commercial license, the project owner must have the legal
right to relicense contributed code. A simple Developer Certificate of Origin does not necessarily
grant those relicensing rights.

Before accepting outside contributions, choose one of:
- a contributor license agreement (CLA) that explicitly grants the necessary rights; or
- a governance model that does not depend on unilateral dual licensing.

Have the final contributor/license documents reviewed before launch.

## Brand moat
Open source permits forks. It does not require granting the SiteWarden name, logo, store publisher
identity or official release-signing identity to forks.

Create a trademark policy that distinguishes:
- official SiteWarden Mail builds;
- compatible community forks;
- prohibited impersonation.

## Official binaries
Do not charge users merely to obtain security fixes. Official binaries should remain available
without a security paywall. Monetize managed services, organizational operation and support.
