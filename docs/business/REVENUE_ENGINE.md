# Revenue engine

## Objective
Maximize sustainable revenue without weakening the open-source/security positioning.

The highest-value customers are organizations that pay for certainty, deployment, integration,
governance, support and licensing — not merely access to the local client.

## Revenue ladder

### Alpha stage
- paid Design Partner engagements;
- controlled Pilot Partner engagements;
- security/deployment professional services;
- strategic sponsorship;
- sponsored development.

### Stable stage
- Community edition remains free;
- managed organization features become commercial;
- enterprise support and administration are contract-based;
- OEM / commercial licensing is negotiated individually.

### Enterprise value
Commercial value can include centralized policy, fleet/device health, managed threat/rule feeds,
SSO/SCIM/SIEM, deployment architecture, contractual support targets, compliance/security
documentation and custom integrations.

### Commercial licensing
Companies that need to embed, redistribute or keep modifications proprietary can request an
alternative commercial license. No public license price is hard-coded; pricing follows scope,
distribution, support and strategic value.

## Sales funnel
Community alpha -> qualified design partner -> controlled pilot -> enterprise/support relationship
-> optional OEM/commercial license.

## Pricing strategy
Do not publish fixed public prices for design partner, pilot, enterprise or OEM work. Qualify the
buyer, understand scope and business value, then issue a written offer.

## Commercial guardrails
- Never claim that Alpha is production-safe.
- Never sell "100% protection".
- Use a written statement of work for paid pilots/services.
- Separate sponsorship from contractual support.
- Track contributor copyright so dual licensing remains possible.
